## Healthcare reporting challenge

Electronic case reporting helps healthcare organizations report reportable conditions to public-health authorities. Each implementation has its own schedule, dependencies, workflows, and data-quality issues, while the delivery work must satisfy clinical, operational, security, and federal requirements.

## My responsibilities

I led electronic case reporting implementation programs for approximately 50 healthcare clients, taking each from kickoff and onboarding through soft and hard go-live. I coordinated schedules, dependencies, change management, stakeholder communication, and data-quality follow-up across engineering, architecture, implementation, client, and public-health stakeholders.

## Delivery approach

I used a repeatable implementation flow: kickoff and onboarding; readiness and approvals; soft go-live for validation; hard go-live; and continued data-quality follow-up. I translated clinical, operational, security, and federal requirements into scoped technical work, and used Agile and Jira practices to maintain visibility across the delivery lifecycle. The implementation context included FHIR and HL7 interoperability standards.

## Approvals, monitoring, and follow-up

Go-live was not the end of delivery. Readiness depended on approval steps, validation, and follow-up for data-quality concerns. I kept stakeholders aligned on what remained, who owned it, and how each dependency affected the next implementation milestone.

## Outcomes and lessons learned

I coordinated approximately 50 healthcare-client implementations. An earlier resume recorded 76% of that portfolio as post-go-live at that reporting point; this is a historical snapshot, not a current completion rate, and was subject to CDC approval and data-quality follow-up. The work reinforced that predictable healthcare delivery requires an explicit shared definition of readiness and disciplined coordination across technical and client teams.
